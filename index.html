🧭 What is archivebox-js?

archivebox-js is a free application that saves complete copies of web pages and stores them on your own computer. Think of it as a "save as" button for the entire internet page — the text, the images, the layout, and the file the page links to, all captured together so it still looks right years from now.

Most web pages change or disappear over time. A product page gets rewritten. A news article is taken down. A recipe blog vanishes. archivebox-js lets you grab a snapshot of that page today, so you can open it later even if the original site is gone or you have no internet connection.

Everything happens inside your browser. There is nothing to configure, no remote server to rent, and no account to create. You install it, you point it at a page, and it saves what it sees.

✨ What you can do with it

  • Save a full page in one click — the text, images, styling, and structure are all kept together in a single archive file.
  • Open your copies offline — view a saved page any time, even with your Wi-Fi turned off.
  • Collect pages in one place — build a personal library of everything you have saved, organized by date and address.
  • Capture more than the visible page — the built-in tooling can follow links, collect page details, and gather related items automatically.
  • Share a saved page with someone else — the archive is a self-contained file you can copy to a USB stick or send to a colleague.
  • Work with your browser extension — run captures directly from the extension, without needing a separate remote server running in the background.

Getting started takes about five minutes. Follow these steps in order and you will be saving pages before your coffee gets cold.

  1. Open the download page. Use the button below. It takes you to the releases page where the application files live.

🖱️ Saving your first page

Once the application is running, saving a page is simple.

  1. Open the web page you want to keep in your browser, exactly as you want it saved.
  2. Use the capture option in your browser or in the app window.
  3. Wait for the progress indicator to finish. Larger pages with lots of images take a little longer.
  4. Open your archive folder to see the finished file. Double-click it to view your saved page.

Each save produces a single archive file that contains the whole page. You can move it, copy it, back it up, or open it on another computer.

🧩 Works with your browser extension

archivebox-js is built to run the full set of archiving add-ons directly inside your browser, so the browser extension can do the heavy lifting on your own machine. You do not need a second computer acting as a server, and you do not need to pay for hosting.

In practice this means one less thing to manage. Install the app, keep it running, and the extension has everything it needs to capture pages reliably.

🔒 Your data stays on your computer

Every page you save is stored locally on your own machine. Nothing is uploaded to a remote service, and no account is required. If you copy your archive folder to an external drive, you have a backup you fully control. This makes archivebox-js a good fit for anyone who wants to keep records without handing them over to a third party.

🛠️ If something does not work

  • The download will not open. Right-click the downloaded file, choose "Properties", and look for a checkbox labelled "Unblock". Tick it, then try again.
  • Windows blocks the app. Click "More info" on the warning screen, then "Run anyway". The app is new, so Windows has not seen it before.
  • A page saves but looks broken. Reload the page in your browser, wait for it to fully finish loading, then capture again.
  • Nothing happens at all. Make sure the application window is still open. If it was closed, start it again and retry.
  • You are not sure which file to download. Go back to the releases page and pick the newest entry at the top of the list.

❓ Questions people ask

Is it free? Yes. There is no cost to download or use it, and no subscription.

Do I need an account? No. There is no sign-up and no login.

Will my saved pages expire? No. Archived files sit on your computer until you choose to delete them.

Can I save a page that requires a login? If you can see the page in your browser, you can generally capture it, because the browser is already signed in.

Do I need to keep my computer on? Only while a capture is in progress. Once a save finishes, the file is complete and self-contained.

🧭 What you will need

  • A Windows computer with a recent version of Windows 10 or Windows 11.
  • A modern browser such as Chrome, Edge, or another Chromium-based browser.
  • About 2 GB of free disk space to start, plus extra room for the pages you save.
  • An internet connection while capturing pages that are online.
